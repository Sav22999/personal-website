Phase 1 · Early Ideation (2023)

Paper Sketches & Mental Models

Born in the Mobile Application Design course at Università degli Studi di Udine to solve everyday food waste.

✏️ Iterating Mental Models

Initial sketches had a generic "Home" screen that caused confusion. After 3 iterations, we split the app into 3 clear pillars: Spaces, Lists & Recipes.

🎯 User Personas

Designed around real needs: busy professionals, university students living alone, and families struggling to track fridge contents.

🌱 Nature-Inspired Palette

Mood board #4 was chosen: natural greens and warm accents symbolizing food freshness, balance, and vitality.

Phase 2 · Proof of Concept

The Academic Flutter Prototype

Building a working mobile PoC for the exam to put our design assumptions to the test.

📱 Coded in Flutter

Brought the Figma wireframes to life with a native Flutter UI, responsive bottom navigation, and action sheets.

⚠️ Static Mock Data Only

No backend, no database. Everything resided in mockData.dart and reset whenever the app closed.

✂️ Features Cut Due to Complexity

Multi-user household sync, camera barcode scanning, and recipe ingredients sync had to be shelved for the exam deadline.

Phase 3 · Usability Testing

What Real Users Taught Us

Testing the prototype with actual users completely shifted our roadmap and priorities.

👥

Shared Households, Not Solo Tracking

Pantry management is rarely an individual task. Flatmates and families needed to share the same spaces and shopping lists in real time.

"I need to share the shopping list and fridge with my flatmates, or we'll buy duplicates."
📷

Frictionless Barcode Scanning

Typing product details manually caused high drop-off. Users needed a quick way to scan items directly with their camera.

"Typing expiration dates and product names one by one takes way too much time."
🍳

Actionable Anti-Waste Recipes

Users didn't just want to see what is expiring; they wanted instant ideas on what to cook with whatever is available right now.

"Tell me what dinner I can make with the spinach expiring tomorrow."
Listening to real users
